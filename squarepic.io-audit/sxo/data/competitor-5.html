Why so many places demand a square

01

Cropped, not squashed

This takes a centred square out of the image. It does not stretch the picture into a square frame, which is the other way of getting to 1:1 and the one that makes everyone look wider than they are.

02

The long sides go

A 16:9 photo loses roughly a third of its width. Whatever matters has to be near the centre — check the edges before you commit, especially on a group photo where someone is standing at the end.

03

Why platforms want it

Square is the only ratio that renders identically in a grid, a list and a circular mask. That is why profile pictures, product thumbnails and post previews converge on it: one asset that cannot break any of the three layouts.

04

No quality cost

Cropping selects existing pixels, so the square is exactly as sharp as the source. Resize afterwards if the platform also specifies dimensions.

05

Transparency is kept

A transparent PNG squares up without gaining a background, so the result still drops onto any colour.

Before you square a photo

Crop the composition first

The square is always centred. If your subject is off to one side, crop it into the middle before squaring or you will lose them.

Square survives every layout

1:1 renders identically in a grid, a list and a circular mask — which is exactly why platforms ask for it.

Square, then resize

Get the ratio right first and the pixel dimensions second. Doing it the other way round means resizing twice.
